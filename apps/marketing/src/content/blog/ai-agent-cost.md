An AI agent costs anywhere from under a dollar per task on a pay-per-outcome plan to several hundred dollars a month on a flat subscription, or five figures and up if you pay someone to build one from scratch. Which number applies to you depends entirely on the job you're buying it for: answering a phone, qualifying a lead, or asking for a review each carry a different price than the generic "AI agent pricing" page most searches turn up. The number nobody prices is what it costs to keep a human approving what the agent does before it reaches your customer, which matters more than the subscription fee once something goes wrong.

## What Are the Different Ways AI Agents Get Priced?

AI agents get priced four different ways, and knowing which one you're looking at matters more than the headline number. The first is pay-per-outcome, where you're charged only when the agent actually finishes a task. Intercom's Fin AI Agent runs this model at $0.99 per outcome, defined as a resolved conversation, a completed workflow, or a case where the customer simply didn't ask for more help. The second is per-seat or usage-based, where you pay for access or activity rather than results. HubSpot's Sales Hub Professional tier, which adds AI-assisted lead scoring on top of the base plan, starts at $90 per seat a month paid monthly, or $100 a month if you commit annually.

The third model is a flat subscription tier, the kind most small businesses actually want: one price, unlimited use within that tier, no per-task math to track. The fourth is a custom build, where you pay a development team to create an agent from nothing. Search for this topic and nearly every result that ranks quotes a custom-build price somewhere between $5,000 and $300,000, because most of what gets written about "AI agent cost" is actually aimed at a company hiring a dev shop, not a small business or an agency looking to buy something that already works. That's the gap this piece is built to close: real numbers for the jobs a small business or an agency actually needs an agent to do, priced against buying, not building.

## What Does an AI Agent Cost to Answer Your Phones?

An AI agent that answers calls runs $0.30 to $0.50 per call on the software side, against $6 to $7.68 for a human agent handling that same call, a gap covered in full in [our breakdown of AI answering service costs](/blog/ai-answering-service). That per-call price covers the software doing the work: picking up, understanding what the caller wants, and either answering, booking, or passing along a message. It doesn't cover what happens the one time in twenty the system mishears a name or books the wrong slot, which is a real cost even when it's not a line item on an invoice.

The honest way to think about this one is volume. A business fielding ten calls a day pays close to nothing extra for an AI agent handling the routine ones, and the dollar gap between $0.40 and $7 a call barely registers at that scale. A business fielding two hundred calls a day is looking at a very different math problem, where the AI agent's software cost replaces what would otherwise be a full-time receptionist's salary, and the savings become the main reason to adopt it rather than a nice side benefit.

## What Does an AI Agent Cost to Qualify a Lead?

An AI agent that scores and routes an inbound lead is usually priced inside a broader sales or CRM platform rather than sold on its own, which is exactly why the sticker price is so hard to pin down. HubSpot's lead-scoring features live behind the Sales Hub Professional tier at $90 a seat a month, and that price gets you the scoring plus the rest of the Sales Hub, not a standalone qualification agent. The alternative most small businesses are actually comparing against is a person doing the sorting by hand. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median wage for a customer service representative at $21.53 an hour, which means every fifteen minutes a rep spends manually sorting a pile of leads costs a little over five dollars in pure labor, before you count what it costs the business when a hot lead sits in that pile too long.

That's the real comparison point, not the software fee by itself. [Our deeper look at how AI lead qualification actually works](/blog/ai-lead-qualification) covers the mechanics, but the cost question comes down to this: a platform charging a flat fee per month pays for itself fast once a business is getting enough inbound volume that a person can no longer eyeball every lead the day it arrives, because the alternative is paying that $21.53-an-hour rate to do manually what the software does instantly, every single time a lead comes in.

## What Does an AI Agent Cost to Ask for Reviews?

Review-request automation sits near the cheap end of the AI agent spectrum, and the pricing is some of the most transparent in this entire space because most of these tools publish a flat monthly rate instead of hiding behind "contact sales". NiceJob's starter plan runs $95 a month for a single location, covering automated review requests sent after a job or appointment, timed to land when the customer is most likely to actually leave one. That flat-fee structure is the norm here: you're not paying per review requested, you're paying for the automation to run continuously in the background.

What that monthly fee doesn't include is the part that actually protects the business: getting the ask right under Google's own rules. And once the reviews start coming in, the responses matter just as much as the requests. ## What Does It Cost an Agency Running This Across Multiple Clients?

This is where the pricing model matters more than the sticker price, and it's also the question almost nothing written about AI agent cost actually answers. Buying three separate point tools for a single client adds up fast once you price each one out: roughly $90 a month for AI-assisted lead scoring bundled into a sales platform, around $95 a month for review-request automation, and $199 a month for a texting and reminder platform like Weave. That lands a single client's tools somewhere around $380 a month before setup fees, and that's without the per-call cost of a separate AI answering service on top. Now multiply that across ten clients and the bill scales in a straight line with every new client you sign, which punishes exactly the growth an agency is trying to create.

A flat-fee white-label platform breaks that pattern. [Our full breakdown of white-label SaaS cost](/blog/white-label-saas-cost) runs the numbers in detail, but the short version applies directly here: a platform like Ownlevel charges $29 to $199 a month depending on the tier, covering lead qualification, review-request, and appointment-reminder agents across every client account within that tier. An agency running ten clients at $300 a month each on the Growth tier pays $99 a month total and keeps the rest as margin, a gap that only gets wider as the agency adds its twentieth and fiftieth client. The alternative, paying per client account or per resolution across three or four separate vendors, means the bill grows every time the agency closes a new deal, which is the one thing a growing agency can least afford from its own tools.

## What Does Keeping a Human in the Loop Actually Cost?

Almost nothing written about AI agent pricing puts a number on this, because most of what ranks for this topic is selling full autonomy as the goal and treating human review as a speed bump to remove. The honest answer is that keeping a person approving outbound actions costs very little in software terms and quite a lot if you skip it. On a flat-fee platform, a human-approval step doesn't add to the bill at all, since it's a workflow setting, not a metered feature. What it costs is a few seconds of someone's attention before a text, an email, or a review reply goes out under the business's name, time that's already accounted for in the labor cost of running the business day to day.

Skipping that step is where the real cost shows up, and it's a cost nobody prices because it only arrives after something goes wrong. An AI agent that quotes a price the business doesn't actually offer, promises a timeline nobody can hit, or replies to a furious customer with a tone-deaf script does damage that takes a human far longer to repair than it would have taken to approve the message in the first place. The businesses that get this right treat the agent's output as a draft a person signs off on, not a decision made unsupervised, and that one habit is worth more than any number on a vendor's pricing page.

## Is Building Your Own AI Agent Cheaper Than Buying One?

Almost never, unless building software is your actual business rather than something you need in order to run it. The custom-build prices that dominate most searches for this topic, $5,000 on the low end and up past $300,000 for a complex system, cover a single agent built for one specific workflow, and that price doesn't include what it costs to keep maintaining, retraining, and fixing that agent once it's live. [Our comparison of white-label cost against building in-house](/blog/white-label-saas-cost) makes the same point about full platforms: a single developer's median salary in the United States is $130,160 a year, and one developer alone can't build and maintain a working AI agent on top of everything else a small team is already responsible for.

The math gets clearer once you separate the two real options a small business or agency actually faces. One is buying a flat-fee platform that already includes the agent types most businesses need, phone answering, lead qualification, review requests, and reminders, for $29 to $199 a month. The other is paying a developer or a dev shop a five-figure minimum to build one agent for one task, then paying again every time that task changes or the underlying AI model gets updated. Building only wins when no existing platform covers the specific workflow a business needs, which is a real but uncommon situation, since most of what a small business or agency actually needs an agent to do, answering calls, scoring leads, asking for reviews, reminding clients, already exists as a proven product somewhere. For almost everyone else comparing these numbers, buying costs a fraction of building and comes with someone else already maintaining it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does an AI agent cost per month?**
It depends on the job. Pay-per-outcome models like Intercom's Fin run about $0.99 per resolved conversation. Flat subscription tiers for a single business function, like review-request automation, typically run $95 to $100 a month. A flat-fee platform covering several agent types at once, like Ownlevel, runs $29 to $199 a month per tier regardless of how many agent types are included.

**Is it cheaper to build an AI agent or buy one?**
Buying is cheaper for almost every small business or agency. A custom-built agent typically starts at $5,000 and climbs past $300,000 for a complex system, plus ongoing maintenance, while a ready-made flat-fee platform costs $29 to $199 a month and already works on day one.

**How much does an AI agent cost compared to a human doing the same job?**
It's usually far less per task, but the comparison depends on the job. An AI agent answering calls costs $0.30 to $0.50 per call against $6 to $7.68 for a human agent. A human sorting leads by hand costs roughly $21.53 an hour at the median customer service wage, a rate an AI agent doing the same sorting instantly undercuts by a wide margin.

**Does an AI agent cost more if a human has to approve its actions?**
No, not on a platform built with that step included. Human approval on a flat-fee plan is a workflow setting, not a separate charge. What costs money is skipping that step and dealing with the damage an unsupervised mistake causes afterward.

**What's the cheapest way for an agency to run AI agents across multiple clients?**
A flat-fee white-label platform, rather than paying per client account or per resolution across several separate vendors. A platform charging $99 a month for up to twenty client accounts keeps an agency's cost flat as it grows, while a per-account or usage-based vendor charges more with every new client signed.

**Why do most AI agent cost guides quote such different numbers?**
Because most of them are answering a different question than the one a small business is actually asking. A search for "AI agent cost" mostly surfaces pages aimed at companies hiring a developer to build a custom agent, priced in the thousands or tens of thousands, rather than pages comparing the ready-made subscription plans a small business or agency would actually buy.
